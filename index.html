Bigger or More? How Has Australia's Protected-Area Network Grown??

Australia has a diverse range of terrestrial and marine ecosystems that require protection to conserve biodiversity and maintain ecological

balance. Its protected areas form an important part of the country's conservation efforts. However, the extent of protection varies across

different environments, jurisdictions and conservation categories. The visualisations explores Australia's protected areas

by examining the distribution, coverage and development of the national conservation network.

III. What Kind of Protection Does Australia Use?

1. International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)

Australia classifies protected areas according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) management categories,

which describe the primary conservation objectives of each area. There are seven categories, and others which are not applicable, not assigned and not reported.

The categories are:
Ia: Strict Nature Reserve
Ib: Wilderness Area
II: National Park

III: Natural Monument
IV: Habitat/ Species Management
V: Protected Landscape/ Seascape
VI: Managed Resource Protected


Which category account for the most protected land?

For terrestrial protected areas, Category II - National Park maintains a relatively stable coverage throughout the decade at around 5%,

while Category Ia - Strict Nature Reserve also accounts for a slightly higher proportion than most other categories.

In contrast, Category VI - Managed Resource Protected is the only category showing an increasing trend, with its coverage rising approximately

every two years. This suggests a gradual increase in terrestrial protection under this management category over the decade.

Which category account for the most protected water?

For marine protected areas, Category II - National Park has the highest coverage over the decade, reaching 20.30%. Meanwhile, Category VI - Managed Resource Protected

maintains relatively stable coverage, fluctuating between 10% and 15%.
In contrast, Category IV - Habitat/Species Management

shows significant growth between 2016 and 2018, with its coverage nearly doubling from 7.94% to 14.57%.

This highlights the differing trends in marine protection across IUCN management categories.
