Bigger or More? How Has Australia's Protected-Area Network Grown??

Australia has a diverse range of terrestrial and marine ecosystems that require protection to conserve biodiversity and maintain ecological

balance. Its protected areas form an important part of the country's conservation efforts. However, the extent of protection varies across

different environments, jurisdictions and conservation categories. The visualisations explores Australia's protected areas

by examining the distribution, coverage and development of the national conservation network.

I - The Growth: How has Australia's Conservation Network Changed?

1. A Growing Conservation Network

This section examines how Australia's terrestrial and marine protected areas have expanded over time, highlighting changes in the extent of land and ocean environments

designated for conservation. By comparing coverage across different years, the visualisations illustrate the development of

Australia's conservation network and reveal differences in the growth of terrestrial and marine protection.

This provides insight into the progress of Australia's conservation efforts and how priorities for protecting natural environments have evolved over time.


Percentage Growth in Protected Area Coverage Across Reporting Years

The visualisation shows an overall increase in Australia's terrestrial and marine protected area coverage over time. Although the growth percentages appear small, they are measured against Australia's total land and ocean area.

In 2020, growth remained positive at 0.0005% for marine and 0.0047% for terrestrial protection, despite appearing close to zero on the visualisation. Marine protection experienced a sharper increase from 2022 to 2024.

Terrestrial protection shows steadier growth, ranging from 0% to 0.10%, while marine protection fluctuates more significantly, reaching approximately 0.4%. This indicates that marine protection

has experienced more pronounced growth in certain years, particularly between 2022 and 2024.
